# Movement Analysis & Technical Lift Auditing

Read this when an uploaded lift video, posture photo, or form check request is provided.

---

## 1. Technical Audit Principles

- **Descriptive, Not Pathological**: Detail joint angles, timing, and bar trajectory objectively (e.g., *"bar drifts 2 inches forward of midfoot during the ascent"* instead of *"your back is breaking"*).
- **External Cues Over Internal Cues**: Athletes respond faster to external cues (e.g., *"push the floor away," "bend the bar across your back"*) than internal muscle cues (*"contract your vastus medialis"*). Limit coaching cues to **1–2 primary actionable cues per lift**.
- **Frame-by-Frame Token Efficiency**: Isolate 2–3 key frames (Setup, Transition/Bottom, Lockout). Store representative screenshots in `logs/<date>/assets/` and describe findings concisely in `session.md`. Never re-analyze identical footage across turns.
- **Camera Angles Required**:
  - *Squat*: 45-degree front-quarter or pure side angle at hip height.
  - *Deadlift*: Pure side angle (bar-path & spine) or 45-degree front angle.
  - *Bench Press*: 45-degree head-side or direct side angle.
  - *If the angle hides key joint checkpoints, request the specific angle before guessing.*

## 2. Compound Lift Biomechanical Checkpoints

### A. The Squat (Back Squat & Front Squat)

```
        O  [Bar over mid-foot]
       /|\
     /  |  \
   /    |    \
 [Torso Angle Matches Shin Angle]
```

1. **Tripod Foot & Stance**:
   - Weight balanced across the heel, 1st metatarsal (big toe base), and 5th metatarsal (pinky toe base).
   - *Cue*: "Grab the floor with your toes and screw your feet into the ground."
2. **Knee Tracking & Valgus**:
   - Knees must track inline with the 2nd/3rd toes throughout descent and ascent.
   - *Fault (Dynamic Valgus)*: Inward knee collapse during the concentric drive out of the hole.
   - *Causes*: Weak hip abductors/external rotators (glute medius), overactive adductor magnus, or collapsed medial foot arches.
   - *Cue*: "Spread the floor apart with the outside of your shoes."
3. **Depth & Pelvic Tilt ("Butt Wink")**:
   - Crease of the hip must drop at least parallel to the top of the patella.
   - *Observation*: Lumbar flexion at terminal depth. Minor pelvic tucking can be anatomical; rapid rounding under heavy axial load increases shear forces.
   - *Corrective Action*: Check ankle dorsiflexion. If ankles are stiff, knees cannot travel forward, forcing excessive hip flexion.
4. **Bar Path**:
   - The barbell must travel in a strict vertical line over the mid-foot. Forward drift indicates early hip rise or forward chest collapse ("good-morning squat").

### B. The Deadlift (Conventional & Sumo)
1. **Starting Position & Mid-Foot Alignment**:
   - Bar placed 1 inch from shins, directly over mid-foot. Scapulae positioned directly over the bar.
2. **Slack Pull & Lat Engagement ("The Wedge")**:
   - Lifter must pull the "click" / mechanical slack out of the barbell before initiating floor push.
   - *Cue*: "Protect your armpits from tickling" or "Bend the bar around your shins."
3. **Spinal Neutrality & Hip Hinge**:
   - Maintain cervical, thoracic, and lumbar alignment. Slight thoracic flexion is acceptable for advanced lifters only if lumbar spine remains rigidly braced.
   - *Fault*: Hips shooting up before the barbell leaves the floor (quad disengagement).
   - *Cue*: "Push the earth away like a leg press."
4. **Lockout**:
   - Finish with glutes contracted and ribcage down. Avoid lumbar hyperextension (leaning back at the top).

### C. The Bench Press
1. **Scapular Stability & Upper Back Arch**:
   - Scapulae firmly retracted (pinched) and depressed (pulled down).
   - *Cue*: "Tuck your shoulder blades into your back pockets."
2. **Grip Width & Wrist Stacking**:
   - Bar rested across the base of the palm (thenar/hypothenar eminence), directly stacked over the radial/ulnar bones. Wrists must not fold back past 15-20 degrees.
3. **Elbow Flare Angle & Touch Point**:
   - Elbows tucked at a 45° to 70° angle relative to torso. 90° flare places excessive anterior capsular strain on the glenohumeral joint.
   - Touch point: Lower sternum / base of chest.
4. **Bar Path (The "J-Curve")**:
   - Barbell descends from over the shoulders down to the lower sternum, then presses diagonally backward toward the face/shoulders during the ascent. A straight vertical path is mechanically inefficient on bench press.
5. **Leg Drive**:
   - Constant isometric leg tension driving backward into the floor, reinforcing the arch without elevating glutes off the pad.

### D. Overhead Press (OHP)
1. **Forearm Verticality**:
   - Wrists stacked over elbows at the start position. Elbows positioned slightly in front of the bar.
2. **Core Bracing & Pelvic Control**:
   - Glutes clamped, abs braced to prevent lumbar lordotic hyperextension during pressing.
3. **Head Clearance & Lockout Window**:
   - Head pulls back slightly during bar ascent, then pushes forward through the "window" as the bar clears forehead level.

## 3. Targeted Corrective Drills Matrix

Match the observed flaw to the specific corrective drill rather than assigning generic mobility work:

| Observed Movement Fault | Root Mechanical Vector | Targeted Corrective Drill |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Squat: Heels lifting / early chest dump** | Restricted ankle dorsiflexion | **5-Inch Wall Ankle Mobilizations** or tempo squats with a 0.5-inch heel elevation plate. |
| **Squat: Inward knee collapse (Valgus)** | Weak glute medius / poor foot arch torque | **Banded Spanish Squats** (isometrics for patellar load) or **Tempo Goblet Squats** with mini-band above knees. |
| **Squat: Stripper squat (hips shoot up early)** | Weak quadriceps relative to posterior chain | **Pin Squats** at sticking point or **Paused Front Squats** (forces upright thoracic posture). |
| **Deadlift: Lumbar rounding off floor** | Inability to brace / weak off-the-floor leg drive | **Deficit Deadlifts** (1-2 inch) or **Paused Deadlifts** (1 inch off floor for 2-second pause). |
| **Deadlift: Bar drifting away from body** | Lat disengagement | **Barbell RDLs with resistance band pulling bar forward** (forces lats to pull bar into shins). |
| **Bench: Elbows flaring out (90°)** | Lack of lat/rotator cuff stability | **Spoto Press** (pause 1 inch off chest) or **Dumbbell Floor Press** with neutral grip. |
| **Bench: Bouncing bar off ribcage** | Loss of eccentric tension | **3-0-3 Tempo Bench** (3 sec down, 0 sec pause, 3 sec up) or **Larsen Press** (feet up, eliminating bounce). |

## 4. Postural Screening (Static Photos)

When reviewing front, side, and rear static posture photos:
1. **Anterior Pelvic Tilt (APT)**:
   - *Visible Signs*: Excessive lumbar lordosis, protruding abdomen, anterior tilt of ASIS relative to PSIS.
   - *Corrective Guidance*: Strengthen rectus abdominis and glutes (**McGill Big 3**, Hip Thrusts); stretch/mobilize tight hip flexors (psoas, rectus femoris).
2. **Upper Crossed Syndrome / Forward Head**:
   - *Visible Signs*: Protracted scapulae, internal shoulder rotation, forward head carriage.
   - *Corrective Guidance*: Strengthen lower/mid trapezius and rhomboids (**Face Pulls with external rotation**, prone Y-T-W raises); mobilize tight pectoralis minor and suboccipitals.
3. **Asymmetric Lateral Shift**:
   - *Visible Signs*: One hip elevated, uneven shoulder height, or lateral weight shift.
   - *Corrective Guidance*: Single-leg unilateral work (**Bulgarian Split Squats**, Single-Leg Romanian Deadlifts, Suitcase Carries) to address bilateral strength deficits.
