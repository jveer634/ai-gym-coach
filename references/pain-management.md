# Pain Management & Movement Regression Protocol

Read this when a user reports pain, joint discomfort, tendon irritation, or movement-related limitation.

---

## 1. Prime Directive: Triage & Non-Clinical Scope

- **You are a coach, not a physical therapist or orthopedic physician.**
- **Never diagnose injuries** (e.g., do not say "You have a torn labrum" or "You have a herniated disc").
- **Triage strictly**: Distinguish between normal adaptation (DOMS), transient joint irritability, and clinical red flags.

### 🚩 Clinical Red Flags (Immediate Referral Required)
If the user reports any of the following, **halt all related loading immediately** and advise consultation with a qualified medical professional (physician, orthopedic surgeon, or physical therapist):
1. **Neurological Symptoms**: Numbness, tingling, "pins and needles," or shooting electrical pain down the arm, leg, or glute.
2. **Structural Trauma**: A distinct audible or tactile "pop" or "snap" followed by immediate swelling, visible deformity, or hematoma/bruising.
3. **Mechanical Joint Lock**: Joint catching, locking, or complete inability to bear body weight.
4. **Systemic Symptoms**: Severe joint effusion, warmth/erythema, or pain accompanied by fever, chills, or night sweats.

---

## 2. DOMS vs. Joint / Tendon Irritation

| Metric | DOMS (Normal Muscle Soreness) | Tendon / Joint Irritation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location** | Diffuse throughout the muscle belly (e.g., middle of quadriceps). | Highly localized to joint line, bone insertion, or tendon (e.g., inferior patellar pole, lateral epicondyle). |
| **Onset** | Peaks 24 to 48 hours post-workout; absent immediately after lifting. | Often felt during the lift or immediately following; sharp or burning sensation. |
| **Response to Warm-up** | Improves or disappears as muscle temperature and blood flow increase. | May dull slightly when warm, but flares significantly post-session or the following morning ("stiff first steps"). |
| **Next Steps** | Light active recovery, normal training progression, hydration. | Execute the Movement Regression Ladder below. |

---

## 3. The 5-Step Movement Regression Ladder

When a lifter experiences sub-clinical joint or tendon discomfort (pain rated $\le 3/10$, returning to baseline within 24 hours), adjust using this systematic hierarchy before abandoning the movement pattern:

```
[Step 1: Setup & Cueing]
         │
         ▼
[Step 2: Load & Velocity]
         │
         ▼
[Step 3: Range of Motion (ROM)]
         │
         ▼
[Step 4: Implement Swap]
         │
         ▼
[Step 5: Isometrics / Tendon Loading]
```

### Step 1: Setup & Cueing Adjustments
- Minor anthropometric tweaks often eliminate impingement or shear.
  - *Squat*: Adjust stance width outward by 1-2 inches or rotate toes out 5-10° to clear anterior hip capsule impingement.
  - *Bench Press*: Narrow grip width slightly to reduce glenohumeral abduction angle; ensure wrists are fully stacked.
  - *Deadlift*: Switch from standard double overhand/mixed grip to trap bar or neutral handles to avoid asymmetrical rotational torque on hips/biceps.

### Step 2: Load & Velocity Reduction
- Reduce working load by **20–30%**.
- Enforce a strict controlled tempo (e.g., **3-1-1-0**: 3-second eccentric, 1-second pause at bottom, 1-second concentric).
- Eliminating ballistic momentum drastically reduces peak tendon shear stress while maintaining muscular hypertrophy stimulus.

### Step 3: Range of Motion (ROM) Modification
- Restrict loading to the pain-free portion of the arc. Never train through a sharp pinch.
  - *Squat Pain at Parallel*: Transition temporarily to **Box Squats** or **Pin Squats** set 1 inch above the symptomatic angle.
  - *Bench Pain off Chest*: Transition to **Floor Press**, **Board Press**, or **Spoto Press** (stopping 1-2 inches above chest).
  - *Deadlift Floor Break Pain*: Transition to **Block Pulls** or **Rack Pulls** elevated just below knee height.

### Step 4: Implement / Biomechanical Swap
- Swap the barbell implement for joints-friendly alternatives:
  - *Barbell Back Squat* $\rightarrow$ **Safety Squat Bar (SSB)**, **Belt Squat**, **Bulgarian Split Squat**, or **Leg Press**.
  - *Straight-Bar Bench Press* $\rightarrow$ **Neutral-Grip Dumbbell Press**, **Swiss Football Bar**, or **Converging Chest Press Machine**.
  - *Barbell Overhead Press* $\rightarrow$ **Half-Kneeling Landmine Press** or **Neutral-Grip Seated DB Press**.
  - *Straight-Bar Biceps Curl* (wrist/elbow strain) $\rightarrow$ **Incline DB Curl with free-rotating wrists** or **EZ-Bar Curl**.

### Step 5: Tendon Loading Protocols (Heavy Slow Resistance / Isometrics)
- Tendons adapt best to high sustained tension with minimal fast stretch-shortening cycles.
  - *Patellar / Quadriceps Tendinopathy*: **Spanish Squat 45-second isometric holds** (3-4 sets) or slow 3-second eccentric leg extensions.
  - *Achilles / Calf Tendinopathy*: **Heavy slow seated & standing calf raises** (3 seconds up, 3-second pause at stretch).
  - *Triceps / Lateral Epicondyle Irritation*: **Cross-body cable triceps extensions with handles** or neutral-grip cable pushdowns.

---

## 4. Re-Introduction Protocol (Return-to-Full-Capacity)

When discomfort subsides:
1. Re-introduce **Volume first** (sets $\times$ reps at lighter load) before increasing intensity.
2. Advance load by no more than **5–10% per week**.
3. Monitor the 24-hour response: If symptoms remain $\le 2/10$ and morning stiffness is absent, continue progression; if symptoms spike $>3/10$, return to the prior successful regression tier for one additional mesocycle.
